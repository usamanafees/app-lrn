UN Regulation No. 152
Uniform provisions concerning the approval of motor vehicles with regard to
Advanced Emergency Braking Systems (AEBS) for pedestrian and cyclist detection

Version: 2024-03 (excerpt for engineering extraction)
Document ID: UN-ECE-R152-2024

================================================================================
1. SCOPE
================================================================================

1.1. This Regulation applies to vehicles of categories M1 and N1 with a maximum
mass not exceeding 3.5 tonnes, equipped with an Advanced Emergency Braking
System (AEBS) intended to detect pedestrians and cyclists.

1.2. The requirements of this Regulation shall not apply to vehicles type-approved
before 1 July 2022 unless otherwise specified in paragraph 1.3.

1.3. Retrofit systems shall comply with Sections 4 through 7 of this Regulation.

================================================================================
2. DEFINITIONS
================================================================================

2.1. "Advanced Emergency Braking System (AEBS)" means a system which can
automatically detect a potential forward collision and activate the vehicle
braking system to decelerate the vehicle with the purpose of avoiding or
mitigating a collision.

2.2. "Critical Object" means a pedestrian or cyclist entering the predicted path
of the subject vehicle such that a collision is imminent if no braking action
occurs.

2.3. "Time-to-Collision (TTC)" means the predicted time interval until contact
between the subject vehicle and the critical object, assuming constant velocity
for both unless sensor fusion indicates otherwise.

2.4. "System Failure" means any condition in which the AEBS cannot perform its
intended function within the performance limits defined in Section 5.

================================================================================
3. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

3.1. The manufacturer shall ensure that the AEBS is operational whenever the
vehicle ignition is in the "ON" state and vehicle speed is greater than 10 km/h
and less than or equal to 60 km/h unless deactivation is explicitly permitted
under Section 3.4.

3.2. The system shall provide a visual warning to the driver at least 1.4 seconds
before the initiation of automatic braking when a critical object is detected
in the forward field of view.

3.3. The system shall log the following events to non-volatile memory:
    (a) activation of automatic braking;
    (b) driver override of automatic braking;
    (c) system failure as defined in 2.4;
    (d) temporary deactivation by the driver.

3.4. Temporary deactivation shall be permitted only when:
    (a) vehicle speed is below 10 km/h; or
    (b) the vehicle is operating in an approved off-road mode documented in the
        vehicle owner's manual.

3.5. Deactivation shall not persist across ignition cycles unless the driver
explicitly confirms deactivation at each new ignition cycle.

================================================================================
4. DETECTION PERFORMANCE
================================================================================

4.1. Pedestrian detection — adult target

4.1.1. The system shall detect an adult pedestrian target crossing the vehicle
path at 5 km/h lateral speed in daylight conditions (illuminance >= 2000 lux)
with a detection rate of not less than 95% over 20 consecutive test runs.

4.1.2. The system shall detect the same target in night conditions (illuminance
<= 5 lux, headlamps ON) with a detection rate of not less than 90% over 20
consecutive test runs.

4.1.3. False positive rate shall not exceed 1 event per 100 km during normal
urban driving as defined in Annex 3, Table A.1.

4.2. Cyclist detection — parallel path

4.2.1. The system shall detect a cyclist travelling parallel to the subject
vehicle at a lateral offset of 1.0 m (+/- 0.1 m) when relative speed is
between 10 km/h and 40 km/h.

4.2.2. Detection shall occur at a minimum distance of 25 m from the subject
vehicle under dry road conditions.

4.3. Sensor degradation

4.3.1. When partial sensor occlusion is detected (>= 30% reduction in effective
field of view), the system shall:
    (a) inform the driver within 3 seconds via a yellow tell-tale; and
    (b) reduce maximum automatic braking deceleration to 4 m/s^2 until occlusion
        is cleared.

4.3.2. The system shall not silently disable automatic braking without driver
notification.

================================================================================
5. BRAKING PERFORMANCE
================================================================================

5.1. Upon detection of a critical object with TTC <= 1.8 s, the system shall
initiate automatic braking to achieve a deceleration of at least 6 m/s^2 on
dry pavement unless limited by Section 4.3.

5.2. Maximum jerk during automatic braking shall not exceed 15 m/s^3 to avoid
loss of driver control.

5.3. The system shall release automatic braking within 500 ms after the driver
applies accelerator pedal input exceeding 50% of full travel.

5.4. Collision speed reduction: for test scenarios defined in Annex 2, the
system shall reduce impact speed by at least 20 km/h or avoid collision entirely.

================================================================================
6. DIAGNOSTICS AND DATA RECORDING
================================================================================

6.1. The system shall store the last 50 AEBS events including timestamp (UTC),
vehicle speed, TTC at warning, TTC at braking, object classification, and
sensor health bitmask.

6.2. Event logs shall be retrievable via the standardized OBD-II interface using
manufacturer-specific mode 0x22 identifiers published in the type-approval file.

6.3. Log retention shall survive loss of primary vehicle power for a minimum of
72 hours using a backup power source or non-volatile storage.

6.4. Personal data shall not be stored in event logs unless required for
homologation and in compliance with applicable privacy regulations.

================================================================================
7. INFORMATION FOR THE DRIVER
================================================================================

7.1. The owner's manual shall describe:
    (a) system limitations in adverse weather;
    (b) meaning of tell-tale indicators;
    (c) procedure for temporary deactivation;
    (d) recommended maintenance intervals for sensors.

7.2. A dashboard tell-tale shall illuminate when the system is temporarily
unavailable due to sensor fault or environmental conditions beyond design limits.
